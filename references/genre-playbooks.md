# Genre playbooks

Read only the section matching the requested deliverable.

## Essay, blog, or general article

Build around a clear thesis or reader promise. Choose an arc—argument, explanation, problem/solution, chronological narrative, or braided reflection—because it fits the material. Use examples to do analytical work, not as decoration. Give the ending a consequence or new vantage point.

Typical failure modes: generic opening, list-like middle, examples without interpretation, repeated thesis, empty inspirational close.

## Academic manuscript or proposal

Map claims to evidence before drafting. Separate established knowledge, the author's results, interpretation, and speculation. Keep terminology stable and calibrate verbs to evidence strength. Make each case or citation answer a local question: what does this evidence establish here, what remains outside its scope, and how does that alter the argument? Synthesis should compare source functions or levels rather than process every source through the same summary-and-caveat routine. For proposals, make the question, gap, approach, feasibility, and expected contribution traceable.

Use installed academic specialist skills for journal-specific conventions, citations, statistics, peer review, or end-to-end paper production when available.

Typical failure modes: novelty by adjective, claims exceeding results, literature laundry lists, identical source-summary-limit paragraphs, method detail without rationale, discussion that repeats results, and polished mini-conclusions that make paragraphs interchangeable.

## Technical documentation or explainer

Define the reader's starting state and target task. Prefer a task path for how-to content and a concept path for explanation. State prerequisites, use tested examples, distinguish required steps from options, and expose failure recovery near the failure point.

Typical failure modes: no audience level, untested examples, hidden prerequisites, mixing tutorial and reference, explaining implementation before purpose.

## Business report, memo, or proposal

Lead with the decision, recommendation, or material finding. Support it with the smallest sufficient evidence chain. Make trade-offs, risks, owners, and next actions explicit. Put background after the decision unless chronology itself is the point.

Typical failure modes: executive summary without a decision, analysis without implication, recommendation without owner or timing, false certainty.

## Marketing copy

Identify one audience, one primary problem or desire, one differentiated value proposition, credible proof, the main objections, and one primary action. Prefer customer language and concrete outcomes. Keep claims honest and supportable.

Typical failure modes: feature wall, vague superlatives, multiple competing calls to action, manufactured urgency, proof disconnected from the claim.

## Personal statement or application

Use specific experiences as evidence of qualities; do not merely name the qualities. Connect past choices to present fit and future direction. Preserve the applicant's authentic perspective and never invent achievements, motives, hardship, or reflection.

Typical failure modes: résumé in paragraph form, generic praise of the institution, cinematic exaggeration, borrowed voice, claims of impact without evidence.

## Narrative nonfiction

Select scenes and details that advance the governing question. Distinguish remembered fact, documented fact, reconstruction, and interpretation. Balance scene with reflection; control pacing through selection rather than invented drama.

Typical failure modes: composite or invented detail presented as fact, chronology without meaning, reflection that over-explains the scene, ornamental description.
