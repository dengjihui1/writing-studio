# Focused editing passes

Choose a quick pass for short, low-stakes text and a full pass for publication, application, assessment, or external-facing work.

## Pass 1: Intent and factual fidelity

Compare the revision against the source. Preserve names, numbers, qualifications, chronology, terminology, citations, and the strength of claims. Flag contradictions and missing support.

Record the permitted content budget before editing. If authority is expression-only, preserve required claims, examples, evidence coverage, qualifications, conclusion, and approximate development. A shorter draft is not automatically a better or more natural draft.

## Pass 2: Structure and argument

Identify the controlling idea, reader journey, and section roles. Move or remove material that obscures the main line. Do not fix a structural problem with transitional phrases alone.

Before preserving the source order, inspect paragraph ancestry: identify whether several paragraphs repeat the same hidden prompt-to-outline-to-paragraph sequence, such as definition, coverage list, balanced qualification, and summary. If the sole draft is highly templated, answer-shaped, or structurally interchangeable, use `first-pass-reconstruction.md` immediately. Recover the source ledger and rebuild section architecture rather than paraphrasing the inherited sentence order.

If a detector report or earlier revision is supplied, read `detector-feedback.md` as an additional evidence path. Compare versions, classify passage deltas, and distinguish layout, surface, structural, and epistemic change. Do not infer causality from an overall percentage alone, and do not make this comparison a condition for deep editing.

## Pass 3: Paragraph function, interior movement, and flow

Give each paragraph one main job and one governing question. Put the topic or turn where the reader can find it. Ensure adjacent sentences relate by cause, contrast, refinement, example, sequence, or consequence.

For substantive or deep rewriting, label sentence functions before editing. Rebuild a paragraph when it repeats the same claim-evidence-qualification-summary sequence found nearby, when its sentences are independently movable, or when it contains a seam left by merging two shorter paragraphs. Paragraph length follows the movement of the thought; never lengthen or shorten prose merely to create statistical variation.

Run the dependency, swap, seam, and closure tests from `human-writing.md`. Adjacent paragraphs should accumulate, contest, narrow, or redirect the argument rather than act as interchangeable cards.

Run a **paragraph ancestry test** on persistent or heavily templated prose. If the paragraph can be reverse-engineered into a generic prompt checklist, or if its sentence order simply mirrors the outline headings, local editing is insufficient. Preserve the evidence ledger, discard the inherited sequence, and rebuild from a narrower live question.

When a comparable detector report shows that a cluster survived the previous substantive rewrite, use the discourse-reset procedure in `detector-feedback.md`. Do not keep paraphrasing the same architecture.

## Pass 4: Clarity and precision

Replace abstraction with concrete wording when the source supports it. Resolve ambiguous pronouns, buried subjects, undefined terms, vague modifiers, and overloaded sentences. Do not manufacture specificity such as invented numbers.

## Pass 5: Voice and tone

Preserve the author's recognizable choices while removing accidental inconsistency. Match formality, confidence, emotional temperature, and technical density to the audience and moment.

If usable writing samples exist, model repeated tendencies rather than isolated quirks: sentence span, vocabulary level, directness, use of qualification, paragraph movement, punctuation, and characteristic ways of opening or closing a thought. Do not copy memorable phrases from unrelated samples.

## Pass 6: Human voice and anti-slop calibration

Read `human-writing.md`. Inspect the whole piece before making local substitutions. Repair repeated templates, empty abstraction, mechanical balance, over-signposting, canned emphasis, generic uplift, and paragraph endings that merely summarize or announce.

Use pattern findings as diagnostic signals, not automatic bans. A passive construction, adverb, dash, three-part list, long sentence, or conventional transition may stay when it improves accuracy, emphasis, rhythm, or genre fit. Never add mistakes, slang, anecdotes, opinions, or biographical detail merely to look human. Do not optimise a word blacklist or sentence-length metric at the expense of reasoning.

## Pass 7: Concision and rhythm

Cut repetition, throat-clearing, empty intensifiers, stacked qualifiers, and sentences that restate the previous line. Keep useful nuance. Let sentence and paragraph lengths vary with conceptual load, and read difficult passages aloud in the target language. Random fragments and forced long-short alternation are not a substitute for cadence.

Also inspect sustained noun-heavy compression, chains of coordinated phrases, and present-participial clauses attached to already dense sentences. These forms may remain when precise; revise them when they hide actors, chronology, or the relative importance of claims.

## Pass 8: Mechanics and format

Check grammar, punctuation, capitalization, spelling variant, headings, lists, quotations, citations, terminology, and requested style guide.

## Review formats

- **Clean copy:** default for ordinary polishing.
- **Before / after / reason:** use for teaching, high-impact changes, or when the user requests explanations.
- **Diagnostic report:** use for critique-only requests; prioritize issues and give representative fixes.
- **Tracked artifact:** use the document-specific skill when the user requests tracked changes or comments in a file.

## Anti-patterns

- Rewriting every sentence into the same polished but generic cadence.
- Paraphrasing highlighted passages sentence by sentence when the underlying reasoning template remains unchanged.
- Merging paragraphs to change appearance while retaining two topic sentences, two setups, or two conclusions.
- Removing all hedging from evidence-bound claims.
- Adding metaphors, drama, or persuasion that the genre does not need.
- Treating passive voice, long sentences, or technical terms as automatically wrong.
- Replacing the author's point with a more fashionable one.
- Returning commentary while withholding the usable revised text.
