# First-pass reconstruction from one draft

## Purpose

Use this workflow when the user supplies only a current draft and asks for deep polishing, humanization, naturalization, or removal of AI-like or formulaic writing. One draft is enough. Do not request a detector report, previous version, or author sample unless it serves a separate user-requested comparison or voice-matching task.

The first revision must be publication- or submission-ready within the supplied evidence boundary. Do not deliberately produce a shallow pass in anticipation of detector feedback.

Lessons learned from later reports may strengthen this first pass, but they must be expressed as editorial decisions that can be diagnosed from one draft. Never turn a successful historical version, score, or report into a prerequisite for a new user.

## 1. Freeze the source boundary

Build a compact ledger before rewriting:

| Unit | Preserve | Question |
|---|---|---|
| facts | names, dates, numbers, events, definitions | is each fact supplied or verified? |
| claims | proposition, scope, causal force, certainty | what is the narrowest faithful formulation? |
| evidence | examples, quotations, cases, data, citations | what does each item actually establish? |
| limits | uncertainty, exception, counterpoint, boundary | where must the argument stop? |
| requirements | prompt, genre, audience, length, format | what must the final deliverable satisfy? |

Do not invent experience, personality, evidence, quotations, citations, or disagreement. Preserve citation placement only when it still matches the proposition supported; otherwise reposition it without changing its intended scope.

Classify the source before drafting:

- **protected:** exact quotations, participant testimony, numbers, proper names, fixed labels, equations, and wording that must remain verbatim;
- **conditional:** methodological terminology, instrument wording, definitions, close source paraphrase, and institutional requirements that may be edited only without changing accuracy or scope;
- **author prose:** synthesis, interpretation, transitions, framing, and conclusions that may be reconstructed within the ledger.

Humanization never authorizes alteration of protected material. Improve how author prose introduces, compares, and interprets it.

Also record the **content budget**: which claims, examples, citations, qualifications, and approximate amount of development the user expects to retain. When the user authorizes expression-level editing only, treat coverage and evidentiary allocation as fixed unless a local change is required for clarity. Do not use compression as a proxy for natural writing. A document can become less formulaic at the same length, or at a slightly greater length, when sentences reveal actors, decisions, evidence relations, and consequences more clearly.

## 2. Diagnose the whole document

Write the controlling idea in one sentence and reverse-outline every paragraph by its actual job. Then inspect for:

- prompt-shaped architecture: the paper visibly follows the order of the assignment rather than the needs of the argument;
- encyclopedic coverage: categories are included to prove completeness but do not change the conclusion;
- equal allocation: weak and central points receive similar space;
- repeated ancestry: paragraphs repeatedly define, list, balance, qualify, and conclude;
- interchangeability: sections or paragraphs can swap positions without damaging the argument;
- citation loops: source summary, safe limitation, broad takeaway repeats across the paper;
- abstraction overload: nominalisations and noun chains hide actors, action, time, or responsibility;
- uniform closure: each paragraph ends with a complete balanced verdict or generic significance claim;
- false flow: transitions announce contrast or causality that the reasoning has not earned.

These signals are sufficient to choose deep mode. Do not wait for a detector to confirm them.

## 3. Select intervention depth

- **Light:** the argument and paragraph dependency are already sound; repair local wording and mechanics.
- **Structural:** the claims are usable but order, emphasis, paragraph jobs, or evidence allocation are weak; reverse-outline and rebuild those relationships.
- **Full reconstruction:** the draft is substantially prompt-shaped, uniformly templated, high-stakes, or composed of interchangeable mini-essays; preserve the ledger but discard inherited sentence order and rebuild the section architecture.

For a long templated draft requested to be humanized, default to structural or full reconstruction. “Polish” in the user's wording does not prohibit necessary structural work when the stated goal is a natural, high-quality result, unless the user explicitly limits edit depth.

An explicit content-preserving or expression-only instruction overrides that default. In that mode, keep the claim ledger, examples, evidence coverage, conclusion, and approximate length stable. Repair local paragraph ancestry, citation function, sentence movement, and transitions without replacing the paper's argument or silently deleting development.

Before a full reconstruction, record the old section-function chain and propose a new one. Reject the proposal if it keeps the same coverage and simply renames the moves. The new chain should alter at least one real dependency: entry point, evidence order, paragraph jobs, allocation of space, or the question that the section resolves.

Also test the proposed section above paragraph level. A revision can discard every old sentence and still reproduce the same answer-shaped sequence if each new paragraph independently introduces a point, processes a source, adds a safe qualification, and closes with a complete verdict. For heavily templated prose, alter at least two of the following where the evidence permits: the first object encountered, the grouping of sources, which paragraph owns a qualification, where the central example receives space, or which implication is carried into the next paragraph.

## 4. Rebuild the argument, not its synonyms

Choose a defensible case, decision, conflict, mechanism, source disagreement, or operational consequence already present in the material as the local or document-level spine. Use it only where it genuinely organizes the evidence.

For each section:

1. state the local question the section must resolve;
2. decide what evidence changes the reader's understanding of that question;
3. ration definitions and background to the point where they become necessary;
4. give central evidence more space than secondary coverage;
5. make each paragraph inherit a problem, term, consequence, or uncertainty from the previous one;
6. let the section end with a changed interpretation, bounded judgement, or concrete consequence rather than a recap.

Do not require every paragraph inside the section to end in a judgement. Where the logic genuinely accumulates, one paragraph may establish evidence or expose a difficulty that the next paragraph interprets. Use this dependency sparingly and make the relation explicit enough for the reader to follow; an unfinished or vague paragraph is not automatically more natural.

Citations should perform different functions where the evidence warrants it: establish a fact, define a disputed term, support a mechanism, qualify a claim, supply a counterexample, or locate a disagreement. Do not fabricate functional variety when the sources do not support it.

For prose that has already survived a substantive rewrite, draft from the ledger with the old prose temporarily out of view. Reopen it only for the fidelity audit. This reduces accidental retention of sentence order, paragraph ancestry, and exhaustive prompt-shaped coverage.

## 5. Reconstruct paragraph interiors

Give each paragraph one governing question. Arrange sentences so that each changes the evidence, actor, scale, condition, confidence, objection, mechanism, or consequence. Run:

- **ancestry test:** does this paragraph repeat the same hidden answer template as its neighbours?
- **dependency test:** can a sentence move elsewhere unchanged? If so, make its local relation explicit or remove it.
- **swap test:** can adjacent paragraphs trade places? If so, rebuild accumulation or contrast.
- **seam test:** is there a second setup or conclusion halfway through? If so, the units were merged, not integrated.
- **closure test:** do consecutive paragraphs end in equally balanced summaries? Replace closure with the specific work needed by the next step.

Merge only when two units answer the same local question. Split at a genuine change in actor, evidence, scale, direction, or problem. Paragraph length must follow conceptual movement; do not target arbitrary uniformity or random unevenness.

## 6. Repair prose after structure

After the argument is stable:

- restore actors and finite actions where noun-heavy compression obscures responsibility or chronology;
- vary sentence span through meaning, subordination, emphasis, and evidence load rather than random alternation;
- remove canned transitions, forced triads, reflexive “not X but Y” contrasts, generic importance claims, and repeated mini-conclusions;
- preserve technical terms, useful hedging, passive voice, parallelism, and long sentences when they perform real work;
- keep the voice restrained and genre-appropriate when no author sample exists; do not manufacture a persona.

## 7. Verify against the sole source

Compare the finished draft with the original ledger and source text. Check every name, number, citation, quotation, causal relation, scope word, degree of certainty, and attributed position. Confirm that no omitted point was required by the brief and no new interpretation is presented as a supplied fact.

Finally run an independent read for:

- argument continuity across paragraphs and sections;
- visible selectivity and unequal emphasis;
- paragraph interiors that develop rather than list;
- natural cadence without deliberate errors or artificial randomness;
- a conclusion that resolves the controlling question instead of inventorying the paper.

Compare the final content budget with the source. A large reduction in explanation requires a substantive reason and user authority; it is not justified merely because the shorter version sounds cleaner or might receive a different detector result.

Deliver the complete revised text first. Mention only material assumptions or evidence gaps. Detector testing, if later supplied, belongs to an optional evaluation loop and does not define whether this first pass is complete.
